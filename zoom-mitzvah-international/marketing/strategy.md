## 1. Positioning

### 1.1 Positioning statement (internal)
**EN:** For Jewish families living outside Israel who want their child (or themselves) to be truly ready for a Bar or Bat Mitzvah and to read from the Torah, **Zoom Mitzvah International** is the private, live course with **Israeli rabbis teaching from Jerusalem in your own language**. Unlike a local tutor or a marketplace teacher, it is a complete path (reading, trope, blessings, Haftarah, tefillin and the speech) with a 24/7 assistant and a library of guides between lessons. All of it is on your schedule, in your time zone.

**FR :** Pour les familles juives vivant hors d'Israël qui veulent que leur enfant soit vraiment prêt pour sa Bar ou Bat Mitsva et sache lire la Torah (ou s’y préparer eux-mêmes), **Zoom Mitzvah International** est le parcours particulier, en direct, avec des **rabbins israéliens qui enseignent depuis Jérusalem, en français**. À la différence d'un professeur local ou d'une plateforme de cours, c'est un accompagnement complet (lecture, té'amim, bénédictions, Haftara, téfilines et dracha), avec un assistant disponible 24 h/24 et une bibliothèque de guides entre les cours. Tout se fait selon votre emploi du temps, à votre heure locale.

### 1.2 The three pillars (in this order)
1. **From Jerusalem.** The rabbis live and teach in Israel. This is the emotional differentiator and the reason to choose us over a local tutor.
2. **In your language.** English or French, both for the child and for the parents. This is the rational differentiator against Israel-based Hebrew-only options and against tutors with weak French.
3. **Everything for the day, and support between lessons.** A complete curriculum plus the AI assistant, recordings, parent portal and 120+ guides. This is the value differentiator against $35-per-lesson options.

Supporting proofs: 1:1 and live; local-time scheduling; patient, adapted teaching (including ADHD/dyslexia); adults welcome.

### 1.3 Core promise (headline system)
- **EN:** *Learn Torah from Jerusalem — without leaving home.*
- **FR :** *Apprenez la Torah depuis Jérusalem, sans quitter la maison.*

This wording is fixed. Use it word for word, with the em dash in EN and the comma in FR, in the hero H1, the closing line, the footer tagline and `og:title`. Do not paraphrase it (e.g., not "Learn Torah in Israel"). The SEO `<title>` is the only exception: it is keyword-led and length-capped (§6.3).

Variants for the child-focused hero (A/B test):
- EN: *Your child's Bar Mitzvah, taught from Jerusalem, in English.*
- FR : *La Bar Mitsva de votre enfant, préparée depuis Jérusalem, en français.*

### 1.4 Claims policy (non-negotiable)
- ✅ Allowed: facts from the brief (1:1, live, rabbis in Israel/Jerusalem, EN/FR, 123 guides in each language, AI assistant 24/7, recordings, parent portal, local-time scheduling, price, and **a free consultation**, per the landing brief's Objective).
- ❌ Not allowed: invented testimonials, star ratings, "X families taught", "the only/first/best", invented rabbi names or credentials, outcome guarantees ("guaranteed perfect reading"), medical/therapeutic claims for ADHD/dyslexia, and "hundreds of articles" without saying "in English and French".
- 🔶 Pending owner confirmation before use: a free trial *lesson* (as distinct from the free consultation), refund policy, number of lessons, Bat Mitzvah Torah-reading scope, which nusach traditions are taught.

## 3. Brand voice

### 3.1 English voice: *"a warm rabbi who is also a great teacher"*
- **Warm, calm, competent.** Short sentences. Concrete nouns ("your Haftarah", "the bimah", "Tuesday at 5:30 p.m. your time").
- **Respectful of every family.** Never assume denomination or observance level. Say "your family's tradition", not "the correct way".
- **Jerusalem as a feeling, not a gimmick.** One evocative line per section at most ("The same stones. Your living room.").
- **Words we use:** prepared, confident, meaningful, your portion, your rabbi, step by step, at your pace.
- **Words we avoid:** "ace", "crush it", "hack", "revolutionary", "world-class", "best", "guaranteed", "cheap", and clinical labels as identities ("ADHD kids" → "children with ADHD").
- Sample: *"Twelve weeks before the bimah is a lot of pressure. Your rabbi breaks it into small, doable steps, and the assistant is there at 10 p.m. when the trope suddenly makes no sense."* (Use only if the owner confirms the course typically runs that long; otherwise drop the timeframe.)

### 3.2 French voice: *« le sérieux d'un rav, la chaleur d'une famille »*
- **Vouvoiement** with parents; tutoiement only in child-facing in-app content (owner's choice).
- **Sobre et chaleureux.** No US-style superlatives, minimal exclamation marks, no anglicisms when a French word exists ("cours particuliers", not "coaching"; "accompagnement", not "mentoring").
- **Community vocabulary:** Bar Mitsva, paracha, té’amim, montée à la Torah, téfilines, dracha, rav. Using these terms naturally signals "on est de la famille".
- **Spelling decisions (FR):** write **« dracha »**, never "drasha", because French writes ש as "ch" (Paracha, Chabbat, Béréchit). Use **« rabbin »** for SEO, headings and first mentions, **« Rav + prénom »** as a title, and **« votre rav » / « son rav »** only in emotional lines and ad hooks. Never use the Hebrew plural "rabbanim" in public copy. *Handoff to Script: update glossary row 17 to « discours (dracha) » and add the rav/rabbin rule.*
- **Respect for minhag:** « selon la tradition de votre famille » appears in the curriculum section.
- **Typography:** non-breaking space before « : ; ! ? », « guillemets », "750 €".
- Sample: *« Le jour de sa Bar Mitsva, il montera à la Torah avec assurance. D'ici là, son rav l'accompagne chaque semaine, depuis Jérusalem, en français. »*

## 4. Landing page: section order, purpose and conversion logic

**Conversion model.** Families research over several weeks, and a cold visitor needs reassurance before paying an overseas company. So the funnel is **consultation first, purchase second**, and the AI assistant is always one tap away. This follows the landing brief's Objective: *"Families abroad book a free consultation or buy the full course. Primary KPI: consultation_booked."*

| Role | Label EN | Label FR | Destination | Event(s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary CTA = primary KPI** | **Book a free consultation** | **Réserver un appel découverte gratuit** (short form in nav and sticky bar: « Appel gratuit ») | Short form (child's age, Bar/Bat Mitzvah date or "not sure", family tradition, language, time zone auto-filled) → available slots shown **in the visitor's local time** → confirmation by email/WhatsApp | `consultation_form_started` → **`consultation_booked`** |
| **Secondary CTA** | **Enroll in the full course** (short: "Get started") | **S’inscrire au parcours complet** (short : « Commencer ») | Same short form → checkout in the geo currency (geo doc §3.4) | `checkout_started` → `purchase` |
| **Always-on helper** | Ask our assistant | Posez votre question à l’assistant | Opens the 24/7 AI assistant (corner launcher plus inline links). It answers objections at 11 p.m., offers the consultation booking inside the chat, and with consent captures an email or WhatsApp | `assistant_open`, `assistant_lead_captured`, `consultation_booked` (source = assistant) |
| **Content CTA** | Find your Parasha / Read the guide | Trouver ma Paracha / Lire le guide | Parasha finder (§5.1), guides (§5.3); every guide ends with the primary CTA | `parasha_finder_used`, `guide_cta_click` |

**Placement rule.** Every CTA block shows the **primary** button. The **secondary** appears only where the visitor has seen the offer: pricing (14), final CTA (16) and the end of the "How it works" section (4). In the hero, the second element is a text link "See how it works" (an anchor, not a competing conversion). That keeps one obvious next step above the fold.

**After `consultation_booked`.** Script owns the confirmation, 24-hour reminder and follow-up messages (`consultation_booked`, `consultation_reminder_24h`, `consultation_follow_up`). The follow-up carries the personal plan summary and the checkout link in the visitor's currency, without pressure tactics.

## 5. Placing the articles on the landing page

The library (123 guides × 2 languages) is the site's biggest SEO and trust asset. On the landing page it does three jobs.

### 5.1 "Find your Parasha" tool (section 10)
- **Input:** the child's date of birth (Gregorian, with an "after sunset" checkbox) **or** a known ceremony date, plus Bar (13) / Bat (12 or 13, user choice) and "Ashkenazi / Sephardi / not sure" (for later personalisation only).
- **Logic:** Convert to the Hebrew date → find the Bar/Bat Mitzvah date → find the Shabbat parasha on or after it, **using the diaspora reading schedule** (Hebcal `@hebcal/core` or the Hebcal API with Israel mode off). Show a short disclaimer: "Always confirm the date with your rabbi or synagogue."
- **Output card:** parasha name (EN transliteration + Hebrew in Frank Ruhl Libre), the Hebrew and Gregorian dates, and links to **our guide for that parasha** (we have all 54), the Haftarah, and the trope and aliyah-blessings guides. Then the primary CTA: "Book a free consultation to start preparing [Parasha]" / « Réservez un appel découverte gratuit pour préparer [Paracha] ».
- **Optional capture:** "Email me this plan" (and the PDF of the guide). Get explicit consent; no pre-ticked boxes (GDPR).
- **Why:** This is the highest-intent moment for a family ("which portion is ours?"). It turns a researcher into a lead and connects directly to content we already have.

### 5.2 Featured guides (section 11)
Six cards chosen for persona coverage, each with a title, a one-line benefit and a reading-time estimate (computed from word count, not guessed):
1. Parents' guide: *How Bar/Bat Mitzvah preparation works, month by month* (from the parents' category)
2. *What is trope, and how does a child learn it?* (trope)
3. *The aliyah blessings, step by step* (torah-reading)
4. *Tefillin: a first guide for families* (tefillin)
5. *Writing the speech: drasha / d'var Torah* (speech)
6. *Nusach: why melodies differ between communities* (nusach)

FR equivalents use French titles written natively, not machine-literal translations (e.g., « Les té'amim expliqués simplement », « Mettre les téfilines : le guide pour les familles »). Exact titles should be pulled from the real library; the ones above are category placeholders.

### 5.3 Contextual article CTAs (throughout)
- Each curriculum item in section 5 gets a quiet text link: "Read the guide →" / « Lire le guide → ».
- Each FAQ answer links to the guide that goes deeper (e.g., the time-zones FAQ → parents' guide; the tradition FAQ → nusach guide).
- Section 12 (learning differently) links to the relevant parents' guides.
- **Inside every guide**, a reverse CTA: an inline box after the first third ("Preparing this portion? Learn it 1:1 with a rabbi in Jerusalem", with a "Book a free consultation" button) and an end-of-article box with the primary CTA plus an "Ask our assistant" link. Clicks fire `guide_cta_click`. The parasha guides carry a parasha-specific CTA.

### 5.4 Library page structure (for SEO)
- `/en/guides/` and `/fr/guides/` hubs, with categories as sub-hubs: basics, parashot (54, ordered by book), tefillin, trope, torah-reading, parents, nusach, speech, tallit.
- One URL per guide per language, `hreflang` pairs EN↔FR (↔ HE on the Hebrew site where an equivalent exists).
- Breadcrumbs plus `Article` schema; `FAQPage` schema only where real Q&A exists.
- Honest counter on the hub: "123 guides, in English" / « 123 guides, en français », or "120+". Say "246 articles" only when the context clearly says "in English and French".

### 6.3 Technical SEO essentials
- Separate URLs per language (`/en/`, `/fr/`), `hreflang="en"`, `hreflang="fr"`, `hreflang="he"` (→ zoomitzvah.co.il equivalent), `x-default` → `/en/`.
- **Never force-redirect by IP**, because Googlebot crawls mostly from the US and would never see `/fr/`. Use a suggestion banner (see geo doc).
- Price in HTML via geo is fine for users, but structured data (`Offer`) should list **both** offers (EUR and USD) with `eligibleRegion`, so the search engine never sees a "wrong" price.

### 6.4 The word "tutor" (and "shul")
"bar mitzvah tutor online" is the #1 money keyword, but the brand calls our teachers **rabbis**, not tutors. Rule:
- **"tutor" is allowed** in meta descriptions, ad keywords and ad copy for search campaigns, and in **one** honest comparison guide ("Bar Mitzvah tutor vs. a rabbi from Jerusalem: how to choose").
- **"tutor" is never used** in brand headlines, the H1, section titles or the hero.
- **"shul"** is allowed in UK/AU/ZA-targeted ads and their landing variants; the neutral "synagogue" is used everywhere else.
- FR equivalents: « professeur de Bar Mitsva » is allowed in SEO and ads only, never in headlines.
