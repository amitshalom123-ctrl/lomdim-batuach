## 1. Positioning

### 1.1 Positioning statement (internal)
**EN:** For Jewish families living outside Israel who want their son to be truly ready for his Bar Mitzvah, and for Jewish men who want to learn Torah themselves (an adult Bar Mitzvah, reading from the Torah, the weekly parasha), **Zoom Mitzvah International** is the private, live course with **Israeli rabbis teaching from Jerusalem in your own language**. Unlike a local tutor or a marketplace teacher, it is a complete path (reading, trope, blessings, Haftarah, tefillin and the speech) with a 24/7 assistant and a library of guides between lessons. All of it is on your schedule, in your time zone.

**FR :** Pour les familles juives vivant hors d'Israël qui veulent que leur fils soit vraiment prêt pour sa Bar Mitsva, et pour les hommes qui veulent étudier la Torah eux-mêmes (Bar Mitsva adulte, lecture de la Torah, paracha de la semaine), **Zoom Mitzvah International** est le parcours particulier, en direct, avec des **rabbins israéliens qui enseignent depuis Jérusalem, en français**. À la différence d'un professeur local ou d'une plateforme de cours, c'est un accompagnement complet (lecture, té'amim, bénédictions, Haftara, téfilines et dracha), avec un assistant disponible 24 h/24 et une bibliothèque de guides entre les cours. Tout se fait selon votre emploi du temps, à votre heure locale.

**Two audiences, one promise.** Parents of boys (P1–P3, P5, P6) and adult men (P4) both buy "a rabbi from Jerusalem, in my language, on my schedule". The difference is who learns and who decides: parents decide for their son; a man decides for himself. Each audience gets its own entry point, section, landing page, keywords and ad angles below.

### 1.2 The three pillars (in this order)
1. **From Jerusalem.** The rabbis live and teach in Israel. This is the emotional differentiator and the reason to choose us over a local tutor.
2. **In your language.** English or French, for your son and for you as parents, or for you as an adult learner. This is the rational differentiator against Israel-based Hebrew-only options and against tutors with weak French.
3. **Everything for the day, and support between lessons.** A complete curriculum plus the AI assistant, recordings, parent portal and 120+ guides. This is the value differentiator against $35-per-lesson options.

Supporting proofs: 1:1 and live; local-time scheduling; patient, adapted teaching (including ADHD/dyslexia); Torah learning for adult men (adult Bar Mitzvah, reading from the Torah, weekly parasha, Torah study).

### 1.3 Core promise (headline system)
- **EN:** *Learn Torah from Jerusalem — without leaving home.*
- **FR :** *Apprenez la Torah depuis Jérusalem, sans quitter la maison.*

This wording is fixed. Use it word for word, with the em dash in EN and the comma in FR, in the hero H1, the closing line, the footer tagline and `og:title`. Do not paraphrase it (e.g., not "Learn Torah in Israel"). The SEO `<title>` is the only exception: it is keyword-led and length-capped (§6.3).

Variants for the son-focused hero (A/B test):
- EN: *Your son's Bar Mitzvah, taught from Jerusalem, in English.*
- FR : *La Bar Mitsva de votre fils, préparée depuis Jérusalem, en français.*

H1 for the adult men's landing page (§4.1, row 13b):
- EN: *Your Bar Mitzvah, your Torah reading: with a rabbi in Jerusalem.*
- FR : *Votre Bar Mitsva, votre lecture de la Torah : avec un rabbin à Jérusalem.*

### 1.4 Claims policy (non-negotiable)
- ✅ Allowed: facts from the brief (1:1, live, rabbis in Israel/Jerusalem, EN/FR, 123 guides in each language, AI assistant 24/7, recordings, parent portal, local-time scheduling, price, and **a free consultation**, per the landing brief's Objective).
- ❌ Not allowed: invented testimonials, star ratings, "X families taught", "the only/first/best", invented rabbi names or credentials, outcome guarantees ("guaranteed perfect reading"), medical/therapeutic claims for ADHD/dyslexia, and "hundreds of articles" without saying "in English and French".
- 🔶 Pending owner confirmation before use: a free trial *lesson* (as distinct from the free consultation), refund policy, number of lessons, which nusach traditions are taught, the adult men's format and price (research §8 Q8), whether men can start from the Hebrew letters (Q9), and father-and-son lessons (Q10).
- ✅ Audience wording: "your son" / « votre fils »; "men" / « hommes » or "adult learners". Never "your child" or wording that suggests a broader audience, and never a sentence about what we don't teach.

## 3. Brand voice

### 3.1 English voice: *"a warm rabbi who is also a great teacher"*
- **Warm, calm, competent.** Short sentences. Concrete nouns ("your Haftarah", "the bimah", "Tuesday at 5:30 p.m. your time").
- **Respectful of every family.** Never assume denomination or observance level. Say "your family's tradition", not "the correct way".
- **Jerusalem as a feeling, not a gimmick.** One evocative line per section at most ("The same stones. Your living room.").
- **Words we use:** prepared, confident, meaningful, your portion, your rabbi, step by step, at your pace.
- **Words we avoid:** "ace", "crush it", "hack", "revolutionary", "world-class", "best", "guaranteed", "cheap", and clinical labels as identities ("ADHD kids" → "if your son has ADHD").
- **Who we address:** parents about "your son"; men directly as "you" (adult copy never talks down, never mentions kids' classes). Never "your child" or "students of all ages".
- Sample: *"Twelve weeks before the bimah is a lot of pressure. Your rabbi breaks it into small, doable steps, and the assistant is there at 10 p.m. when the trope suddenly makes no sense."* (Use only if the owner confirms the course typically runs that long; otherwise drop the timeframe.)

### 3.2 French voice: *« le sérieux d'un rav, la chaleur d'une famille »*
- **Vouvoiement** with parents and with adult men; tutoiement only in in-app content for boys (owner's choice). Write « votre fils », « les hommes », never « votre enfant ».
- **Sobre et chaleureux.** No US-style superlatives, minimal exclamation marks, no anglicisms when a French word exists ("cours particuliers", not "coaching"; "accompagnement", not "mentoring").
- **Community vocabulary:** Bar Mitsva, paracha, té’amim, montée à la Torah, téfilines, dracha, rav. Using these terms naturally signals "on est de la famille".
- **Spelling decisions (FR):** write **« dracha »**, never "drasha", because French writes ש as "ch" (Paracha, Chabbat, Béréchit). Use **« rabbin »** for SEO, headings and first mentions, **« Rav + prénom »** as a title, and **« votre rav » / « son rav »** only in emotional lines and ad hooks. Never use the Hebrew plural "rabbanim" in public copy. *Handoff to Script: update glossary row 17 to « discours (dracha) » and add the rav/rabbin rule.*
- **Respect for minhag:** « selon la tradition de votre famille » appears in the curriculum section.
- **Typography:** non-breaking space before « : ; ! ? », « guillemets », "750 €".
- Sample: *« Le jour de sa Bar Mitsva, il montera à la Torah avec assurance. D'ici là, son rav l'accompagne chaque semaine, depuis Jérusalem, en français. »*

## 4. Landing page: section order, purpose and conversion logic

**Conversion model.** Families research over several weeks, and a cold visitor needs reassurance before paying an overseas company. So the funnel is **consultation first, purchase second**, and the AI assistant is always one tap away. This follows the landing brief's Objective: *"Families abroad book a free consultation or buy the full course. Primary KPI: consultation_booked."*

**Two funnels, one booking flow.** The same consultation flow serves both audiences; only the entry copy and the first form question differ.
- **Parents of boys:** landing page `/en/`, `/fr/` → hero path card "For my son" → consultation (or checkout). Ads angles 1–7 (§7).
- **Adult men:** dedicated page `/en/adult-bar-mitzvah/`, `/fr/bar-mitsva-adulte/` (row 13b) plus section 13 on the main page → "For myself" → consultation. Ads angles 8–12 (§7), the adult keyword clusters (§6), and the parasha guides' adult CTA (§5.3).
- **Budget split:** start with roughly two-thirds of test budget on parents of boys and one-third on adult men per language, then move budget to the lower cost per **qualified** consultation after 2–4 weeks `[starting hypothesis, no baseline]`.

## 5. Placing the articles on the landing page

The library (123 guides × 2 languages) is the site's biggest SEO and trust asset. On the landing page it does three jobs.

### 5.1 "Find your Parasha" tool (section 10)
- **Input, two tabs:** **"My son's Bar Mitzvah"** / « La Bar Mitsva de mon fils »: his date of birth (Gregorian, with an "after sunset" checkbox) **or** a known ceremony date, plus "Ashkenazi / Sephardi / not sure" (for later personalisation only). **"For me"** / « Pour moi »: "This week's parasha" (no input) or a chosen Shabbat for an adult Bar Mitzvah.
- **Logic:** Convert to the Hebrew date → add 13 Hebrew years to find the Bar Mitzvah date → find the Shabbat parasha on or after it, **using the diaspora reading schedule** (Hebcal `@hebcal/core` or the Hebcal API with Israel mode off). ### 6.3 Technical SEO essentials
- Separate URLs per language (`/en/`, `/fr/`), `hreflang="en"`, `hreflang="fr"`, `hreflang="he"` (→ zoomitzvah.co.il equivalent), `x-default` → `/en/`.
- **Never force-redirect by IP**, because Googlebot crawls mostly from the US and would never see `/fr/`. Use a suggestion banner (see geo doc).
- Price in HTML via geo is fine for users, but structured data (`Offer`) should list **both** offers (EUR and USD) with `eligibleRegion`, so the search engine never sees a "wrong" price.
- **Title rule: every `<title>` is ≤ 60 characters**, counting spaces and the "| Zoom Mitzvah" suffix. The build should check this and fail on anything longer. In FR SEO titles, use "rabbin", not "rav".

| Page | EN `<title>` | Chars | FR `<title>` | Chars |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Landing page | Online Bar Mitzvah Lessons from Jerusalem \| Zoom Mitzvah | 56 | Cours de Bar Mitsva en ligne depuis Jérusalem \| Zoom Mitzvah | 60 |
| Adult men's page (13b) | Adult Bar Mitzvah & Torah Reading for Men \| Zoom Mitzvah | 56 | Bar Mitsva adulte et lecture de la Torah \| Zoom Mitzvah | 55 |
| Weekly parasha / Torah study page (Phase 2) | Weekly Parasha & Torah Study, 1:1 Online \| Zoom Mitzvah | 55 | Paracha de la semaine avec un rabbin \| Zoom Mitzvah | 51 |
| Guides hub | Torah Guides: Parasha, Trope, Tefillin \| Zoom Mitzvah | 53 | Guides de Torah : Paracha, té’amim, téfilines \| Zoom Mitzvah | 60 |
| Parasha guide (template) | Parashat {Name}: Bar Mitzvah Guide \| Zoom Mitzvah | 43 + name | Paracha {Nom} : guide de Bar Mitsva \| Zoom Mitzvah | 45 + nom |

So the name may be up to 17 characters in EN and 15 in FR. For longer or combined parashot (e.g., "Acharei Mot-Kedoshim", 20 characters), drop the "Parashat " / "Paracha " prefix so the title stays ≤ 60.

- **Meta descriptions (≤ 155 characters):**
  - Landing EN (149): "Private 1:1 Zoom lessons with Israeli rabbis in Jerusalem who speak English. Bar Mitzvah prep for your son, Torah reading for men. Free consultation."
  - Landing FR (152) : « Cours particuliers sur Zoom avec des rabbins francophones à Jérusalem : la Bar Mitsva de votre fils, la Torah pour les hommes. Appel découverte gratuit. »
  - Adult men's page EN (134): "Adult Bar Mitzvah, Torah reading, weekly parasha: private 1:1 lessons with a rabbi in Jerusalem who speaks English. Free consultation."
  - Adult men's page FR (139) : « Bar Mitsva adulte, lecture de la Torah, paracha de la semaine : cours particuliers avec un rabbin de Jérusalem, en français. Appel gratuit. »
- `og:title` is **not** the SEO title. It is the fixed promise (§1.3), because social shares favour emotion over keywords.

### 6.4 The word "tutor" (and "shul")
"bar mitzvah tutor online" is the #1 money keyword, but the brand calls our teachers **rabbis**, not tutors. Rule:
- **"tutor" is allowed** in meta descriptions, ad keywords and ad copy for search campaigns, and in **one** honest comparison guide ("Bar Mitzvah tutor vs. a rabbi from Jerusalem: how to choose").
- **"tutor" is never used** in brand headlines, the H1, section titles or the hero.
- **"shul"** is allowed in UK/AU/ZA-targeted ads and their landing variants; the neutral "synagogue" is used everywhere else.
- FR equivalents: « professeur de Bar Mitsva » is allowed in SEO and ads only, never in headlines.
